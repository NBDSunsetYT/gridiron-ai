## Continued model findings

See [model audit](MODEL_AUDIT_V17.md), [calibration plot](reports/calibration-v17.png), and the versioned JSON artifacts. The app's existing model-audit screen and read-only `/api/engine?view=calibration` endpoint expose the compact findings. The research assistant's read-only experiment tool includes that evidence.

- **342 archived development games** reanalyzed: **124 NFL, 174 CFB, 44 NBA**, spanning 2024–2026 calendar dates across separate cohorts. These are not 342 newly simulated games. The older archives remain descriptive comparisons rather than silently pooled calibration training data.
- **36 engine/calibration configurations** explored with expanding chronological folds; temperature scaling, regularized Platt scaling and isotonic regression. Three NBA category/position mean-regularization settings were also diagnostic candidates. No candidate was promoted.
- On 24 later CFB development games, score MAE improved **17.198 → 10.208–10.679**, but home-win Brier worsened **0.1669 → 0.1729–0.1789**. Favorite confidence increased and several probability shifts favored eventual losers. Improved score levels and interval coverage did not validate winner reliability.
- NBA minute grading matched **447 player-game records across 24 games**: reference MAE **6.451 minutes**, RMSE **8.177**, bias **−2.393**. Existing challengers barely changed these errors. These correlated player rows are not independent games. Original pregame injury/starter coverage remains insufficient for causal attribution.
- The calibration sweep found no statistically credible qualifying improvement across the available windows. The already inspected final period was excluded from new fitting and selection. No new untouched-test accuracy result is claimed.

The candidate grid finished without a qualifying improvement. Promotion remains blocked by limited independent windows, small suitable training samples and historical availability gaps. Compute budget was not exhausted. Further tuning against exposed final outcomes would not establish generalization. All original forecasts and final-test reports remain preserved.

The successful main calibration pass used approximately **1.12 local CPU seconds**, the legacy descriptive pass **0.77 seconds**, plus the separately reported minute-audit CPU. These figures exclude test/build/other session work. New research Monte Carlo runs, provider requests and hosted simulation CPU were **zero**. Small synthetic mechanical regression tests do not count as real-game backtests.
